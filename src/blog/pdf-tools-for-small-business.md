Small businesses live in PDFs. Invoices from suppliers. Contracts with clients. Receipts for taxes. Proposals and quotes. Employee documents.

You need to merge, split, convert, and protect these files regularly. The wrong tool can expose client data to a third-party server. Here's a privacy-conscious toolkit.

## Merge PDFs: Combine Documents for Clients

**Use case:** Bundle a proposal, terms sheet, and pricing appendix into one file for a client.

**Privacy note:** Proposals often contain pricing, strategy, and client names. Don't upload them to a random merge website.

**Solution:** Browser-based merge — drag files, reorder, download. No upload.

## Split PDFs: Extract What You Need

**Use case:** A client sends a 40-page contract; you only need the signature pages. Or you need to pull one invoice from a monthly statement PDF.

**Privacy note:** Contracts and financial statements are confidential.

**Solution:** Split locally — select page ranges, download only what you need.

## Image to PDF: Digitize Receipts and Scans

**Use case:** Photograph receipts with your phone, combine into one PDF for your accountant. Scan signed documents into a single file.

**Privacy note:** Receipts show vendors, amounts, and sometimes payment methods. Tax documents are highly sensitive.

**Solution:** Convert images to PDF in your browser. Reorder pages, download one file.

## Encrypt PDF: Protect Before Sending

**Use case:** Email a contract or financial report. Add a password so only the recipient can open it.

**Privacy note:** Encrypting on a server means the unencrypted file was uploaded first.

**Solution:** Password-protect locally, then send the encrypted file and share the password through a separate channel.

## PDF to Image: Use Pages Elsewhere

**Use case:** Pull a diagram from a PDF for a presentation. Convert a signed page to an image for a website or social post.

**Solution:** Convert pages to PNG without uploading the source PDF.

## Why Small Businesses Should Care

- **Client confidentiality** — NDAs and professional ethics require protecting client documents
- **Tax and financial data** — receipts and statements attract identity thieves
- **Competitive information** — proposals and pricing shouldn't leak
- **Regulatory compliance** — some industries have data handling requirements

A "free" online tool with a vague privacy policy is a liability.
