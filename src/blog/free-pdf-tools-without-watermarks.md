You merge three PDFs on a free website. The download works — but every page has a logo stamped in the corner. "Remove watermark: $9.99/month."

This is the business model of many "free" PDF tools. The free tier is a demo. The real product is a subscription.

## The Watermark Trap

Common patterns:

- **Watermark on output** — your merged PDF has the tool's branding on every page
- **Page limits** — "Free for up to 3 pages" or "5 merges per day"
- **File size caps** — "Maximum 10MB" unless you upgrade
- **Quality reduction** — compressed output only for paid users
- **Account required** — "Sign up free" that leads to upsell emails

You do the work, then discover the result isn't usable without paying.

## Why Tools Do This

PDF processing on servers costs money — storage, bandwidth, compute. Watermarks and limits convert free users into paying customers. It's a valid business model, but it's not "free" in any meaningful sense.

## What Actually Free Looks Like

Truly free tools:

- **No watermark** on output
- **No page or file count limits** (within reasonable device memory)
- **No account** required
- **No premium tier** hiding basic features

Browser-based tools can offer this because they're not paying for server processing — your device does the work. The cost is yours (electricity, CPU time), not theirs.

## Open Source as a Signal

Open-source PDF tools rarely watermark output — there's no business incentive to. The code is public; adding hidden paywalls would be visible and unpopular.

If a tool is open source and runs in your browser, you can verify there's no watermark logic in the output.

## Privacy Bonus

Truly free browser-based tools also tend to be more private — no upload means no server storing your files while they upsell you.

Upload-based "free" tools have your file on their server. That's another reason to prefer local processing.
