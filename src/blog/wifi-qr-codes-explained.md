"What's the WiFi password?"

You read out `K7#mP9xL2nQ4vR8w` while your guest types it wrong three times. There's a better way: a QR code that connects them automatically.

## How WiFi QR Codes Work

A WiFi QR code encodes your network name (SSID) and password in a standard format. When someone scans it with their phone camera:

1. The phone reads the encoded credentials
2. It prompts to join the network
3. One tap — connected. No typing.

The format is standardized (WIFI:T:WPA;S:YourNetworkName;P:YourPassword;;) so it works on iPhone and Android.

## When to Use WiFi QR Codes

- **Home** — print a QR code for the guest room or fridge
- **Office** — lobby or meeting room signage
- **Events** — conference badges or table tents
- **Airbnb / rental** — welcome packet for guests
- **Cafes and shops** — counter display for customer WiFi

## Security Considerations

A WiFi QR code contains your actual password. Anyone who scans it can connect. That means:

- **Don't post it publicly** online or in windows visible from the street
- **Use a guest network** if your router supports one — separate from your main network
- **Change the password** if you suspect the QR was shared too widely
- **Regenerate the QR** when you change the password

For home guest networks, the convenience usually outweighs the risk. For business networks with sensitive data, use a dedicated guest SSID with limited access.

## Creating a WiFi QR Code

You need:

- **Network name (SSID)** — exactly as it appears in WiFi settings
- **Password** — the WPA/WPA2 key
- **Security type** — usually WPA or WPA2 (WEP is outdated)

A QR generator takes these inputs and produces a scannable image. Customize colors and size for printing.

## Print and Display Tips

- **Minimum size:** 2×2 cm (about 1 inch) for reliable scanning
- **High contrast:** dark code on light background
- **Test before printing** — scan with your own phone first
- **Laminate** for high-traffic areas like cafes

## Keep Credentials Local

Your WiFi password is sensitive. Generating a QR code on a website that uploads data to a server means your network credentials travel over the internet.

Browser-based QR generation creates the code on your device. Your password never leaves your computer.
