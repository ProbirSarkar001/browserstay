QR codes went from novelty to utility. Restaurants link to menus. Business cards link to portfolios. Products link to manuals. Events link to registration.

Creating one takes seconds. Using it well takes a bit more thought.

## What to Link To

**High-value destinations:**

- **Website or landing page** — your main site, a product page, a promo
- **Menu** — PDF or web menu for restaurants and cafes
- **Contact info** — vCard so scanning saves your phone and email
- **WiFi** — guest network credentials (see our [WiFi QR guide](/blog/wifi-qr-codes-explained))
- **Social profiles** — Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube
- **Payment** — PayPal, Venmo, or tip links
- **App download** — App Store or Play Store link
- **Feedback form** — Google Forms, Typeform, survey

**Avoid:** linking to pages that change often without updating the QR. A dead link on printed material is worse than no QR.

## Size and Placement

- **Minimum scan size:** about 2×2 cm (1 inch) for reliable phone camera reading
- **Distance:** larger codes for posters viewed from farther away
- **Contrast:** dark code on light background (black on white is safest)
- **Quiet zone:** leave empty margin around the code — don't crop tight
- **Flat surface:** curved bottles and wrinkled fabric are harder to scan

## Customization

Many generators let you:

- Change foreground and background colors (stay high-contrast)
- Add a logo in the center (don't cover too much of the code)
- Export as PNG (raster) or SVG (scalable for print)

Test every customized code with multiple phones before printing 500 business cards.

## Print Checklist

1. Generate the code
2. Scan with your phone — confirm it opens the right URL
3. Scan from the intended distance (across a table, from a poster wall)
4. Print a test copy before bulk printing
5. Use SVG or high-resolution PNG (300 DPI for print)

## Dynamic vs Static QR Codes

**Static:** the URL is baked into the code. Change the destination = new QR code = reprint everything.

**Dynamic:** a short URL redirects to your real page. You can change the destination without reprinting. Usually requires a paid service that hosts the redirect.

For printed materials with long lifespans (business cards, packaging), static is simpler. For campaigns you might update, dynamic can help — but adds a dependency on a third-party redirect service.

## Privacy When Generating

QR codes for business often encode URLs with tracking parameters, or WiFi passwords, or contact details. Generating on a website that logs inputs means your business data goes to their server.

Browser-based generation creates the code on your device. Your URLs and credentials stay local.
