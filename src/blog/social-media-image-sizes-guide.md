You designed a perfect banner. Uploaded it to LinkedIn. The platform cropped off your headline. You re-exported at a random size from a blog post dated 2019. Still wrong.

Social platforms change specs occasionally, but the core dimensions are stable enough to plan around. Here's a practical reference — and how to hit these sizes without sending your creative assets to a random resize website.

## Why Size Matters

Platforms resize and crop uploads to fit their layouts. Upload the wrong aspect ratio and the algorithm decides what to cut — usually the edges you cared about most.

Upload oversized files and the platform compresses them anyway — often worse than if you'd optimized first. Upload undersized files and they get upscaled, looking soft and pixelated.

The goal: **correct dimensions, reasonable file size, sharp quality.**

## The Safe Universal Sizes

If you don't want to memorize a table:

- **Landscape link preview:** 1200 × 630 (works on Facebook, LinkedIn, X)
- **Square content:** 1080 × 1080
- **Vertical story/reel:** 1080 × 1920
- **YouTube thumbnail:** 1280 × 720

Design at these dimensions and you cover most use cases.

## Resize Workflow

1. **Design at target size** — best option if you're creating from scratch
2. **Export large, resize down** — if you have a high-res original, scale down (never up)
3. **Crop to aspect ratio** — if dimensions don't match, crop before resize
4. **Compress** — JPEG quality 80–85 for photos; PNG for graphics with text
5. **Convert format** — WebP for web embedding; JPEG/PNG for platform uploads

Marketing images, unreleased product photos, and client work shouldn't be uploaded to random "free resize" websites. Those files end up on someone else's server — sometimes indexed, sometimes retained.

Browser-based resizing processes files on your device. Your campaign assets stay yours.

## File Size Tips

Platform upload limits are usually generous (Instagram allows up to 30MB for photos), but smaller files upload faster and load quicker for viewers.

- Resize to exact platform dimensions — don't upload 4000px images
- Compress JPEGs to quality 80–85
- Use PNG only for graphics with text or transparency
