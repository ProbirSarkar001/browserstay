You have a 50-page report but only need pages 12–15 for a meeting. Or a scanned document where each page should be a separate file. Or a PDF with a blank page you want to remove.

Splitting a PDF — extracting specific pages into new files — is one of the most common PDF tasks. Here's how to do it privately.

## What Splitting Actually Does

A PDF is a container of page objects. Splitting doesn't re-render or recompress anything — it creates a new PDF that references only the pages you selected. That's why it's fast and why it works entirely in your browser.

## Common Splitting Scenarios

### Extract a page range

You need pages 5–10 from a 30-page document. Select that range, export, and get a new 6-page PDF.

### Split every page into separate files

A 20-page scan becomes 20 individual PDFs — useful for filing, emailing single pages, or uploading to systems that accept one page at a time.

### Remove unwanted pages

Select everything except page 7 (the blank one) and export. Same result as deleting a page.

### Pull one chapter from a book

Academic papers, manuals, and ebooks often need one section extracted. Pick the page range and save.

## How to Split Without Uploading

1. **Open a browser-based PDF splitter** — one that processes files locally
2. **Load your PDF** — it stays in your browser's memory
3. **Select pages** — by range, individually, or "split all"
4. **Download** — one file or a ZIP of multiple files

No upload progress bar. No waiting for a server. Your document never leaves your device.

## Tips for Clean Results

- **Check page numbers** — PDF page numbers and printed page numbers sometimes differ (cover pages, front matter)
- **Password-protected PDFs** must be unlocked before splitting
- **Large scanned PDFs** may take a moment to load — that's normal for big files
- **Batch splitting** — if you need many ranges, do them in one session rather than reloading the file each time

## Split vs Merge: When to Use Which

- **Split** — one PDF → multiple PDFs (or one smaller PDF with fewer pages)
- **Merge** — multiple PDFs → one combined PDF

Often you'll do both: split a large document, merge selected pages with another file, then encrypt the result.
