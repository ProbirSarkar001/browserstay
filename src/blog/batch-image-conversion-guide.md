You have 200 iPhone photos in HEIC format. You need them all as JPEG for a photo book. Or 50 PNG screenshots that need to become WebP for your website. Or a folder of mixed formats that should all be AVIF.

Converting one image at a time is painful. Batch conversion fixes that.

## What Batch Conversion Does

Batch conversion applies the same settings to every image in a set:

- **Input:** multiple files (or a folder)
- **Output format:** JPEG, PNG, WebP, AVIF, etc.
- **Quality:** same level for all (e.g., 85%)
- **Output:** individual files or a single ZIP download

One operation, consistent results.

## Common Batch Scenarios

### iPhone photos to JPEG

Export an entire camera roll or album. HEIC → JPEG for Windows, printing, or sharing with non-Apple users.

### Screenshots to WebP

Developers and designers often have folders of PNG screenshots. Batch convert to WebP for documentation sites — smaller files, same quality.

### Mixed formats to one format

A project folder with JPG, PNG, and WebP scattered around. Batch convert everything to a single format for consistency.

### Prepare images for a website

Convert to WebP or AVIF, set quality to 80, resize to max 1200px — all in one workflow.

## Settings That Matter

- **Output format** — match your destination (email = JPEG, web = WebP/AVIF)
- **Quality** — 80–85 for photos, 90+ if you need near-lossless
- **Dimensions** — resize during conversion to avoid a second step
- **Naming** — some tools preserve original names with new extensions

## ZIP vs Individual Downloads

For 5–10 files, individual downloads are fine. For 50+, a ZIP saves time and keeps your downloads folder organized.

## Privacy for Personal Photos

Batch converting often means hundreds of personal images — vacations, family, home. Uploading that entire folder to a website sends your entire photo library to a stranger's server.

Browser-based batch conversion loads files into your browser's memory, processes them locally, and offers a ZIP download. Nothing leaves your device.
