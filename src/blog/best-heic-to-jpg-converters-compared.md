## Server-Upload Converters

**iLoveIMG, CloudConvert, HEICtoJPEG.com** and similar sites ask you to upload your photos.

**Pros:**
- No install required
- Work on any device with a browser
- CloudConvert supports many output formats
- Usually handle batch uploads

**Cons:**
- **Your personal photos are uploaded** — family pictures, home photos, documents you photographed
- **Privacy policies** claim deletion, but you can't verify
- **Free tiers have limits** — CloudConvert caps free conversions per day
- **Quality settings** often limited on free tiers

**Best for:** A single non-personal image when you don't care about privacy.

## Desktop: CopyTrans HEIC (Windows)

Installs a codec and right-click "Convert to JPEG" in Windows Explorer.

**Pros:** Fully offline, integrates with File Explorer, batch via folder selection
**Cons:** Windows only, installation required, limited format options

**Best for:** Windows users who convert HEIC regularly.

## Built-In: iPhone Settings

**Settings → Camera → Formats → Most Compatible** makes new photos JPEG.

**Pros:** No conversion needed going forward
**Cons:** Doesn't convert existing HEIC files; larger storage usage on phone

**Best for:** Preventing the problem rather than fixing it.

## Mac: Photos App Export

Select photos → File → Export → export as JPEG.

**Pros:** Built-in, offline, batch export
**Cons:** Mac only, multi-step process

**Best for:** Mac users with photos already in the Photos library.

## Why Privacy Matters for Photo Conversion

HEIC files are almost always personal — vacation photos, kids, pets, your home, documents you snapped with your phone. Uploading hundreds of these to a free converter sends your entire camera roll to a server.

Browser-based conversion treats photos the way you'd expect: they stay on your device until you choose to save the result.
