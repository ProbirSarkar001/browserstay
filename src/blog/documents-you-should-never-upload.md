Free online tools are convenient. Drag, drop, download — done. But convenience has a cost when the document matters.

Here are five categories of files you should never upload to a random website — and what to do instead.

## 1. Tax Returns and Financial Statements

Tax documents contain your Social Security number, income, bank details, and employer information. A single leaked tax return is enough for identity theft.

**What to do:** Use desktop software or a browser-based tool that processes files locally. Merge, split, or compress tax PDFs without uploading them.

## 2. Medical and Health Records

Prescriptions, lab results, insurance claims, and doctor's notes are protected health information in many countries. Uploading them to an unvetted server may violate privacy laws — and puts your health data in unknown hands.

**What to do:** Keep medical PDFs on your device. Use local tools for merging visit summaries or compressing scanned records for email.

## 3. Signed Contracts and Legal Agreements

NDAs, employment contracts, leases, and settlement documents often contain confidential terms. Uploading them to a free tool may breach the confidentiality clause you're trying to honor.

**What to do:** Process legal PDFs locally. Merge exhibits, split pages for redaction, or add password protection — all without a third party seeing the content.

## 4. Identity Documents

Passports, driver's licenses, national ID cards, and visa applications are high-value targets for fraud. Never upload these to any service unless it's an official government portal you trust.

**What to do:** If you need to combine ID scans for an application, use offline or browser-based tools. Convert images to PDF on your device before sharing through secure channels.

## 5. Unreleased Business or Product Information

Internal reports, unreleased designs, client lists, and proprietary data don't belong on someone else's server — especially a "free" one with a vague privacy policy.

**What to do:** Use tools that run on your machine. Open-source browser tools let you verify that files aren't transmitted.

## The Common Thread

All of these documents share one property: **if a stranger read them, you'd have a problem.**

Upload-based tools require you to trust that stranger. Browser-based tools remove that trust requirement — your files never leave your computer.

## A Simple Rule

Before uploading any file, ask: *"Would I email this to someone I don't know?"*

If the answer is no, don't upload it. Use a tool that works locally.
