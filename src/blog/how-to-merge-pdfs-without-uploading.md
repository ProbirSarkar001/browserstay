Combining multiple PDFs into one file is one of the most common document tasks. Invoices, reports, scanned pages, signed contracts — they often need to become a single document before you email or archive them.

The problem: most "merge PDF" websites ask you to upload every file to their server first. Here's how to merge PDFs without ever sending them anywhere.

## Why Uploading Matters

When you upload a PDF to merge it online, you're giving a copy to a third party. Even if they promise to delete it after an hour, you have no proof. For personal, financial, or business documents, that's a real risk.

Browser-based merging avoids this entirely. Your files stay on your device from start to finish.

## Method 1: Browser-Based Tool (No Install)

This is the fastest option if you don't want to install software.

1. **Open a browser-based PDF merger** — look for one that explicitly says files are processed locally
2. **Add your PDF files** — drag and drop them into the tool. They load into your browser's memory, not a server
3. **Reorder if needed** — drag pages or files into the order you want
4. **Merge and download** — the tool combines the PDFs on your device and gives you a single file to save

The entire process happens in your browser. No upload progress bar, no "processing on our servers" message.

## Method 2: Built-In OS Tools

**On macOS:** Open the first PDF in Preview, then drag additional PDFs from Finder into the sidebar. Reorder thumbnails, then export or save as a single PDF.

**On Windows:** The built-in PDF support is limited. Microsoft Edge can view PDFs but doesn't merge them natively — a browser tool or free desktop app is usually easier.

## Method 3: Desktop Software

Apps like Adobe Acrobat, PDF-XChange Editor, or free tools like PDFsam work offline. Your files never leave your machine. The downside is installation and sometimes cost.

## Tips for a Clean Merge

- **Check page order** before merging — it's easier to fix now than after
- **Use consistent page sizes** if possible — mixing A4 and Letter works but can look uneven
- **Password-protected PDFs** need to be unlocked first before merging
- **Large files** — browser-based tools handle most sizes fine; very large scans (100+ MB) may be slower on older devices

## What Happens Technically

PDF merging doesn't re-render pages. A PDF is a container of page objects — merging means building a new container that references pages from each source file. That's why it's fast and why it can run entirely in your browser.
