You scanned five pages of a form. You have a folder of product photos that need to become a catalog. You took screenshots of receipts and want one file for your accountant.

Converting images to PDF is one of the most practical document tasks — and it doesn't require uploading anything to the cloud.

## Why Images to PDF?

- **Email one file** instead of ten attachments
- **Print in order** — PDFs preserve page sequence
- **Archive scans** — combine related images into one document
- **Submit applications** — many forms accept PDF but not loose images
- **Share professionally** — a single PDF looks more polished than a ZIP of JPGs

## Supported Image Formats

Most converters accept:

- **JPEG** — photos, scans
- **PNG** — screenshots, graphics with transparency
- **WebP** — modern web images
- **HEIC** — iPhone photos (may need conversion first on some systems)

Mix formats freely — one PDF can contain JPG pages and PNG pages.

## Page Order Matters

When combining multiple images, order is everything. A good tool lets you:

- **Drag and drop** to reorder before converting
- **Add more images** after the first batch
- **Remove pages** you added by mistake

Get the order right before generating the PDF — reordering a finished PDF is another step.

## Page Size and Margins

Images come in different dimensions. When building a PDF:

- **Fit to page** — scale each image to fit a standard page (A4, Letter)
- **Original size** — preserve each image's dimensions (good for mixed sizes)
- **Margins** — add padding around images for a cleaner look

For scanned documents, "fit to page" usually works best. For photo collections, original size or uniform scaling looks more natural.

## Quality and File Size

PDFs with many high-resolution photos can get large. If size matters:

- Resize images before converting (1200px wide is often enough for documents)
- Use JPEG source images rather than PNG when possible
- Compress the final PDF if your tool supports it

## Keep Scans Private

Scanned IDs, medical forms, and financial documents shouldn't be uploaded to random websites. Browser-based conversion loads your images into memory, builds the PDF locally, and lets you download — nothing transmitted.
