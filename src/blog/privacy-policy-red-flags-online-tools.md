Before uploading a sensitive PDF or photo to a free online tool, you might glance at the privacy policy. Most people don't — they're long, legal, and boring.

But a few phrases reveal a lot. Here are red flags to watch for.

## Red Flag 1: "We May Retain Files for [Vague Period]"

**What it means:** Your file might be stored longer than "immediately after processing."

**Better:** "Files are processed in your browser and never uploaded" or "Files are deleted within [specific short time] and we do not retain copies."

**Why it matters:** "May retain" gives them legal room to keep your data. Vague timelines are meaningless.

## Red Flag 2: "We Collect Usage Data and Analytics"

**What it means:** They track what you do — which tools you use, file sizes, how long you spend, your IP address.

**Better:** Minimal or no analytics, especially for privacy-focused tools. Or analytics that don't tie to file content.

**Why it matters:** Metadata about your document (size, type, timestamp) can be sensitive. "We don't store your file" doesn't mean "we don't log that you processed a 47-page PDF at 2am."

## Red Flag 3: "Files Are Processed on Our Servers"

**What it means:** Upload required. Your file travels to their infrastructure.

**Better:** "Processed locally in your browser" or "client-side processing."

**Why it matters:** Server processing = they can see your file. Period.

## Red Flag 4: "We May Share Data With Partners"

**What it means:** Your information (and possibly file metadata) can be sold or shared with third parties — advertisers, analytics companies, "partners."

**Better:** "We do not sell or share your data" with a clear list of any subprocessors (e.g., hosting provider only).

**Why it matters:** "Partners" is a catch-all. Ad networks are partners. Data brokers are partners.

## Red Flag 5: "By Using Our Service, You Grant Us a License to..."

**What it means:** You might be giving them rights to use your uploaded content — for "improving our services," "training," or "creating derivative works."

**Better:** No license to user content, or explicit statement that uploaded files are not used for any purpose beyond providing the service.

**Why it matters:** Some terms of service allow using your documents to train AI or improve products. Your contract draft could end up in a training dataset.

## Red Flag 6: No Privacy Policy at All

**What it means:** They didn't bother, or they're hiding something.

**Better:** A clear, readable privacy policy that matches what the product actually does.

**Why it matters:** If they won't tell you what they do with data, assume the worst.

## Red Flag 7: "We Use Cookies for Advertising"

**What it means:** The tool is ad-supported. Your behavior feeds ad targeting.

**Better:** No ads, or ads without behavioral tracking. Privacy-respecting analytics only.

**Why it matters:** Free tools often monetize through ads. Your file-processing habits become ad profile data.

## What Good Looks Like

A privacy-respecting tool should be able to say:

- Files are not uploaded (client-side processing)
- No accounts required
- No sale of data
- Open source (verifiable)
- Minimal analytics, if any
- Clear contact for privacy questions
