You need a slide from a PDF as an image. Or a scanned page as a PNG for editing. Or every page of a report as JPG files for a website.

Converting PDF to image is straightforward — but most online converters upload your PDF first. Here's how to do it privately.

## Why Convert PDF to Images?

- **Presentations** — embed a PDF page as an image in PowerPoint or Google Slides
- **Social media** — share a document page as a post or story
- **Editing** — open a PDF page in Photoshop, GIMP, or Canva
- **Thumbnails** — create preview images for a document library
- **Archiving** — store individual pages as image files

## JPG vs PNG for PDF Conversion

- **JPG** — smaller files, good for photos and complex pages. Some quality loss.
- **PNG** — lossless, sharper text and graphics. Larger files. Best for documents with text, diagrams, or screenshots.

For scanned documents and pages with lots of text, PNG usually looks better. For photo-heavy PDFs, JPG saves space.

## Resolution Matters

PDF pages are vector-based — they can be rendered at any resolution. Higher DPI = sharper images but larger files.

**Practical targets:**

- **Screen use (web, slides):** 150 DPI is usually enough
- **Printing:** 300 DPI
- **Thumbnails:** 72–96 DPI

Converting at 600 DPI for a web thumbnail wastes space without visible benefit.

## Batch Conversion

A 20-page PDF can become 20 images in one go. Look for tools that:

- Convert all pages at once
- Let you pick JPG or PNG
- Download as a ZIP when there are many pages

## The Upload Problem

PDFs often contain sensitive content — contracts, financial data, personal information. Uploading to a conversion website sends that content to a server you don't control.

Browser-based conversion renders each page in your browser and exports images locally. The PDF never leaves your device.
