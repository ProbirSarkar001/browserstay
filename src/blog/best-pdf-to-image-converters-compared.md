## Server-Upload Converters

**iLovePDF, Smallpdf, Adobe, PDF24, Sejda** all require uploading your PDF before conversion.

**Strengths:**
- Fast on powerful servers
- Adobe offers TIFF output and fine DPI control
- PDF24 is generous on free tier
- Mature, tested rendering engines

**Weaknesses:**
- **PDF uploaded to third-party server** — contracts, financial reports, medical records exposed
- **Smallpdf: 2 tasks/day**
- **Sejda: 3 tasks/hour, 200-page limit**
- DPI control often limited on free tiers

**Best for:** Public documents, marketing PDFs, non-sensitive content.

## Browser-Based: BrowserStay

Renders PDF pages to JPG or PNG in your browser using PDF.js.

**Strengths:**
- PDF never leaves your device
- Convert all pages or selected ranges
- Download individually or as ZIP
- No daily limits, no account

**Weaknesses:**
- Very large PDFs (100+ pages) take longer on older devices
- No TIFF output
- Requires initial page load

**Best for:** Confidential documents, client files, anything you wouldn't email to a stranger.

## Resolution Guide

| Use case | Recommended DPI |
|----------|----------------|
| Web / social media | 150 DPI |
| Presentations | 150–200 DPI |
| Printing | 300 DPI |
| Thumbnails | 72–96 DPI |

Higher DPI = sharper images but larger files. For screen use, 150 DPI is usually plenty.

## JPG vs PNG for PDF Conversion

- **JPG** — smaller files, good for photo-heavy PDF pages
- **PNG** — lossless, better for text, diagrams, and screenshots

For scanned documents and pages with lots of text, PNG usually looks sharper.
